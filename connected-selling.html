01

The context

An enterprise customer reconsidering its collaboration and CRM tools wanted to make a connected workflow tangible for its internal teams at a leadership event. The brief was a storytelling demo: bring collaboration, sales and service workflows, and an internally built agent together in one experience.

We had approximately 1–1.5 weeks to create a click-through for a demo video, with one script connecting multiple products and personas.

02

My contribution

I drove visual fidelity and structure. When reviews flagged differences in spacing, headers, icons, and threaded conversations, my focus was bringing the screens closer to familiar product patterns while keeping the multi-product story coherent.

I drew on the platform team's UI guidance rather than inventing a separate visual language. Another designer focused on interaction design, timing, voice-overs, and micro-interactions.

03

The problem

Sellers lacked a single place to use their organization's AI tools, and CRM access sat outside the collaboration workspace. The customer also wanted threaded channels and chats. Our challenge was to show how these disconnected pieces could form a familiar, connected way of working.

04

The demo story

The eight-scene story followed an opportunity from its appearance in a channel through meeting preparation and a client call, ending with the CRM updating itself.

Before / Starting workflow

CRM outside the workspace

Information and workflows were separated across applications.

After / Demo concept

CRM within the workspace

The demo proposed CRM access alongside collaboration, connecting customer context with preparation and follow-through.

The connected state was explored in the demo, not released as a feature.

05

Designing within constraints

  • Complexity: six products and agents, multiple personas, and one narrative to follow.
  • Authenticity: experienced users needed to recognize the product, not just understand the story.
  • Time: multiple feedback rounds a day, coordinated across product teams, customer stakeholders, and time zones.
06

The pivot

Initial issue

The first prototype lacked authenticity

The story was present, but product experts spotted unfamiliar spacing, headers, icons, and conversation patterns.

Pivot in approach

Ground the demo in the real product

We changed our approach: partner with the platform design team, use its repositories and UI guidance, and reuse established patterns. Detailed reviews, down to border radius, became part of the process.

Observed improvement

Reviewers noticed the difference

Reviewers familiar with earlier versions commented on the progress and increased authenticity.

A full rebuild was considered. With team support, we instead iterated on the existing prototype, incorporating customer terminology and workflow requests without losing the script. This was a pivot in approach, not a restart.

Believability also depended on behavior: loading states, a join sound, and a pause before the meeting complemented the visual work.

07

Outcome & takeaways

The requesting customer's representative thanked the team for bringing the workflow to life. They also highlighted the escalation-support portion and expected it to resonate with stakeholders.

Feedback on the demo, not measured adoption or confirmation of event results.

What I took forward

  • Details build trust. Visual and behavioral cues make a concept believable.
  • Expertise enables iteration. Product-team guidance helped us improve rather than restart.
  • Clear roles support speed. Shared reviews and complementary responsibilities kept the work moving.
