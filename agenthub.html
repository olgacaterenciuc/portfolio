01

The brief

Enterprise admins wanted to adopt Copilot and AI agents, but documentation, governance guidance, and activation steps were disconnected. The brief was to bring understanding and action into one admin experience: learn what AI does, understand its requirements, and plan a rollout.

02

Learn: context before action

My Learn work brought technical and governance guidance into the hub rather than leaving admins to assemble answers across separate sources. The landing page connects AI learning resources with entry points for rollout planning, credit calculation, and agent insights.

03

Adopt: make rollout deliberate

I designed Rollout Manager as a staged activation flow, separating the choice of agents and capabilities from their configuration. The stepper keeps progress and the later review-and-publish stage visible.

1. Choose what belongs in the rollout

Agent selection and capability checkboxes make rollout scope explicit before detailed setup. Admins can choose which capabilities to include rather than treating an agent as an all-or-nothing switch.

2. Check readiness and configure behavior

For case follow-up and closure, the flow surfaces prerequisite status and rule selection together. It keeps rule selection in context and links to rule editing rather than embedding another full editor. I worked with engineering to align the experience with existing systems.

04

Choosing the scope

I reviewed legacy work, reframed the problem with PMs, and compared how other platforms centralized guidance and controls. Many answers to admin concerns already existed; the opportunity was to connect them to the adoption journey.

We started with Learn, Adopt, Track, and Verify. We deferred Verify from this phase and renamed Track to Measure, giving the team a shared three-pillar structure.

06 / Reflection

This project reinforced the value of involving engineering early and making scope decisions explicit. My focus was not just organizing information, but connecting what admins need to understand with the decisions they need to make.
